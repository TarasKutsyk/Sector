# Check My Summary

For explaining the system back in the owner's own words and finding out what is actually understood. Standalone, or the closer after a lesson or a sabotage hunt. When enabled, it should also be offered at the end of a major planning session, with questions aimed where the understanding gap is likely largest: the mechanics least discussed so far, the ones central to the plan, or the ones that only make sense when several pieces are stitched together. The agent uses its judgement on where that gap is.

## Play

1. Agree the target: a report, a plan, a module, or the whole workstream, and how many minutes.
2. The agent prepares, privately, the list of expected concepts from the sources: the parts, how they connect, and the two or three invariants that matter. For a planning session it instead prepares two to four targeted questions on the least-discussed or most load-bearing mechanics.
3. The owner explains, from memory, in prose or in a sketch, or answers the targeted questions.
4. The agent grades against the sources and sorts every point into: correct, wrong, or left unsaid. A wrong claim and an omission are never mixed up.
5. For one crucial omission the agent asks a targeted follow-up and lets the owner answer before the final grade.
6. The agent reports the result: understood, or needs another pass, with the specific remaining gaps and the source line for each.

## Rules

- Understanding, never recall of trivia. Names of files and fields do not count as concepts.
- Score not only "concepts named", but how accurately the owner described "how the concepts connect". Mixed details are also mistakes.
- No compulsory memorization, no automatic offer after every session.
- The summary and its grading are genuine analysis: they go with normal artifacts, not the games dir.

## What you get

The three lists, the follow-up exchange, the verdict with its gaps. Then the score line per `SCORING.md`: concepts named minus wrong claims, follow-up restores.

## Files

`artifacts/<date>_summary_<target>.md` in the workstream, holding the owner's words verbatim and the grading. Score row in the scoreboard.
